abstract: "*Aims.* Understanding the internal structure of HII regions is fundamental to constraining star-formation processes in galaxies. We investigate how the *filling factor* (FF) relates to luminosity, size, electron density, and H&alpha; equivalent width in HII regions. We also explore the association between star-forming regions and PAH-to-dust emission, to assess their possible connection to the evolutionary stages of HII regions. <br> *Methods.* We investigate the FF in 622 HII regions in NGC 628, combining 475 from SIGNALS and 147 from PHANGS-MUSE, using a dedicated analysis pipeline. We derived luminosity, emission-line fulxes, radii, electron densities, and FF. We used PHANGS-JWST survey images to study the spatial relationship between the FF and PAH-to-dust emission, quantified by the PAH-to-dust ratio R<sub>PAH</sub> using JWST/MIRI emission bands at 7.7, 11.3, and 21 &mu;m. <br> *Results.* Higher Log(FF) and high EW(H&alpha;) values are found in luminous regions, whereas more extended regions with lower EW(H&alpha;) display lower Log(FF) values. We show that the HII radius definition significantly affects the L<sub>H&alpha;</sub>--R relation. Low-luminosity, compact HII regions appear to reflect a transition from cluster-powered regions to nebulae ionized by single massive stars with the transition occurring around log(L<sub>H&alpha;</sub>) &sim; 37 erg s<sup>-1</sup>. The PAH-to-dust ratio R<sub>PAH</sub> correlates with the volumetric H&alpha; luminosity density, L<sub>H&alpha;</sub>/R<sup>3</sup>, with a transitional range centered around log(L<sub>H&alpha;</sub>/R<sup>3</sup>) &sim; 32 erg s<sup>-1</sup>, corresponding to log(FF)&nbsp;&asymp;&nbsp;-4.4. Regions with lower Log(FF) values exhibit higher R<sub>PAH</sub> values, suggesting less efficient PAH processing in more porous structures. <br> *Conclusions.* These results could be consistent with an evolutionary scenario in which the FF decreases with stellar cluster age in giant HII regions as they evolve toward fainter and more spatially extended states. The volumetric H&alpha; luminosity density, L<sub>H&alpha;</sub>/R<sup>3</sup>, provides a useful representation that reduces the covariance between L<sub>H&alpha;</sub> and R induced by different region definition methods, enabling more consistent cross-catalog comparisons across segmentation approaches."
